The contract

The guest drives two byte-wide ports and sleeps on one interrupt line. Everything above the byte channel — framing, the eight sockets, JOIN/OPEN/SEND/STATUS — is the ESP32's job and matches sys/NET.md and the simulator's nic.c.

PortAddressReadWrite
NETDATA0x60next RX FIFO byteone TX FIFO byte
NETSTAT0x61RXRDY 0x01 TXRDY 0x02 RDY 0x04ignored

Interrupt. The card pulls /VI5 low while the RX FIFO holds a byte and releases it when the guest drains the FIFO. It is an open-collector, level-asserted line: the 7406 sinks it, a 4.7 kΩ pull-up restores it. VI5 is S-100 edge pin 9, also brought out on the proto board at P23.8.

Board versions. The layout below is checked against the V3.0a KiCad artwork (the 2017 release). The V2 board has the same P40–P48 headers and the same 74LS682, but its 74LS682 Q jumpers are three-pin GND/Q/VCC shunts; the V3 board uses two-pin shunts to GND and relies on the 682's internal Q pull-ups. Strap tables give both.

Logical layout

The proto board already buffers the S-100 address, data and control lines in 74LS245s and decodes the port in a 74LS682. The card adds only what the module needs: a 5 V to 3.3 V receive path, the 3.3 V data-in drive into the board's DI buffer, and the open-collector interrupt driver.

One trace cut

The 682 compares A7…A0, with A0 on P7 (pin 17). To make it match both 0x60 and 0x61, lift U110 pin 17 from the A0 net and wire it to ground; fit JP4 (Q7 = 0). Without this, the card answers 0x60 only. If you will not cut a trace, a second 74LS682 or a 74LS688 dead-bugged on the proto area can decode A7…A1 instead; the rest of the layout is unchanged.

On a V2 board the Q jumpers are three-pin: shunt pins 1–2 for Q = 0, pins 2–3 for Q = 1. The fit/open table above is the V3 equivalent. The 682 sees only A0–A7, so the upper I/O byte is ignored, as on any 8-bit S-100 card. If your system pages I/O with A8–A15 and another board also answers 0x60 in a different page, add a second comparator on those lines.

Write latch and 6 MHz

At 6 MHz the Z80's I/O write window is only about 250 ns, too tight to trust a firmware poll of DO0b–DO7b. Latch the byte in hardware. U23 is a 74LS373: D0–D7 from P54.1–P54.8, Q0–Q7 to U20 A0–A7, LE from U24, /OE grounded, VCC = +5 V. The D inputs get 10 kΩ pull-downs so they are defined when U109 is disabled. U24 is a 74LS00 that makes LE = sOUTb AND SEL*, so only our port is captured.

The latch is transparent while sOUT is high and holds the byte when it falls. The ESP32 detects the falling edge of sOUTb with SEL* low and reads the held byte through U20 at its own speed. At 6 MHz the strobe is about 250 ns — 60 cycles at 240 MHz — so core 0 sees it, and the data is stable before and after.

03

Level translation

The board's “buffered” pads are 74LS245 outputs running from a 5 V rail. That matters: an LS high is 2.7 V minimum, about 3.4 V typical, not 5.0 V. The ESP32's input-high threshold is 0.75 × 3.3 V ≈ 2.48 V, and its absolute maximum on a GPIO is 3.6 V.

A 2:1 divider from an LS high lands near 1.7 V, below the ESP32's threshold; the earlier note that a divider gives “about 2.5 V” assumed a 5.0 V source that these pads do not provide. Do not use 10 kΩ/10 kΩ dividers here.

Use a 74LVC245 at 3.3 V

Two DIP-20 74LVC245 transceivers, VCC = 3.3 V on pin 20 from the module's 3V3, pin 10 to ground. Their inputs are 5 V tolerant to 5.5 V, their input-high threshold is 2.0 V at 3.3 V, and their outputs swing 0–3.3 V into the ESP32 with ±24 mA drive. Use each as a one-way buffer: DIR (pin 1) to 3.3 V for A→B, /OE (pin 19) to ground. The A side is the latch's Q0–Q7 on U20 and the board's buffered pads on U21; the B side is the ESP32. U20 carries the latched Q0–Q7 on A0–A7; U21 carries sOUTb, sINPb, SEL* and A0b on A0–A3. Tie U21's spare A4–A7 (pins 6–9) to ground and leave its B4–B7 open.

The pinout is the familiar '245: DIR 1, A0–A7 2–9, GND 10, B7–B0 11–18, /OE 19, VCC 20. The B side runs backwards — B0 is pin 18 and B7 is pin 11 — which is the easy wiring mistake.

Never put an LVC245 in one of the board's 5 V buffer sockets. These two are new positions in P14, powered from 3.3 V; U101–U109 stay on the 5 V rail. The 5 V tolerance is specified with VCC present; if the board can be powered with the module removed and the 5 V bus live, 1 kΩ series resistors on the twelve A inputs limit the clamp current.

If you would rather use the through-hole part, two CD4050BE hex buffers at VDD = 3.3 V are the alternate: inputs tolerate 18 V, VIH is about 0.7 × VDD ≈ 2.3 V, outputs are 0–3.3 V, and they are slower. The signals are the same, but the pinout and the P14 sockets differ (DIP-16).

The other direction needs no translator. The ESP32's 3.3 V high is above the 74LS245's 2.0 V VIH, so DI0b–DI7b drive U107's B-side inputs directly. The 7406 is also a TTL-threshold part, so GPIO12 drives its input directly.

Add 10 kΩ pull-downs on DO0b–DO7b. U109's B side follows the S-100 DO lines, which float when no write is in progress; the pull-downs keep the LVC245 A inputs defined and keep the ESP32 from reading noise. GPIO5 and GPIO15 are strapping pins: GPIO5 is one of the DI outputs, so fit a 10 kΩ pull-up from that module pad (H10) to 3.3 V; GPIO12 is the interrupt output, so fit a 10 kΩ pull-down from L1 to ground. GPIO15 is unused.

Do not use an LVC245 for VI5; it is push-pull, and the interrupt line needs the open-collector 7406 (or a 74HCT06).

Physical layout

The board is the standard 254 × 127 mm (10 × 5 in) S-100 card, with the 100-pin edge tongue along the bottom. The top half is twelve 15 × 15 PGA wire-wrap sockets in two rows; the port decode and the bus buffers sit along the bottom. The ESP32 goes in the top row, its antenna toward the card's top edge, and the level shifters go in the bottom row directly above the data headers.

Mechanical notes

Antenna. The module's PCB antenna sits at its top end. In the P7 socket it overhangs the board edge by only about 4 mm; the rest of the keepout is over the P7 pads. That is enough for bench work and marginal in a metal S-100 chassis. If the card lives in a metal box, either mount the module on standoffs 10–15 mm further toward the top edge and feed it with a short 2×15 ribbon, or use an ESP32-WROOM-32U with an external antenna on a bulkhead connector. Do not put a metal panel or the card guide directly over the antenna.

USB. The connector is at the module's lower end, over the P13 area. Program the module before the final assembly or leave a right-angle cable path; P13 is empty for exactly this reason.

Height. The module sits on 8.5 mm female headers. Its lower end overhangs P13 by about 10 mm, clearing the empty socket. Do not install a wire-wrap socket in P13.

Wiring. Use 30 AWG wire-wrap for the bus signals and keep the DO/DI runs short. The SEL* run from P47/U110 to P14 is the longest at about 130 mm; it is a level, not a strobe, so it is not timing-critical. Twist the VI5 run with a ground return if the chassis is noisy.

Mounting. The module's own holes are 2.8 mm, 43.9 mm apart; nylon standoffs are optional once the headers are soldered.

Power

The board's U1 LM7805 is the only regulator the card needs. Feed it from the S-100 +8 V rail and let the module make its own 3.3 V:

S-100 +8 V (pins 1, 51)
   └─ P70 1–2 ─→ U1 LM7805 ─→ +5 V (VCC rail)
                                ├─ 74LS buffers, 7406, pull-ups
                                └─ DOIT VIN (O1) ─→ AMS1117-3.3 ─→ 3.3 V
                                                                     ├─ ESP32
                                                                     └─ LVC245 VCC
  • Fit U1 and jumper P70 1–2; fit K12 1–2 for U1's ground. Leave U2/U3 and their input jumpers unpopulated; the card uses no ±12 V.
  • Budget about 360 mA at 5 V: ~120 mA of LS logic, ~240 mA ESP32 peak during WiFi transmit. The 7805 drops about 3 V at that current, so fit a small TO-220 heatsink; +8 V is unregulated and can sit near 7 V at the low end, which is marginal for a 7805 at full WiFi current. If your +8 V rail is weak, feed the module's VIN from a separate 5 V supply for bench work.
  • Decoupling: 470 µF/10 V at the module VIN, 100 nF at VIN and 3V3, 100 nF at each LVC245, 100 nF at the 74LS373 and 74LS00, and 100 nF at the 7406. The ESP32's transmit bursts are the reason for the bulk capacitor.
  • The '245 pinout: pin 20 VCC (3.3 V), pin 10 GND, pin 1 DIR, pin 19 /OE. Do not power them from +5 V. The latch and the gate run from +5 V.

Bring-up

  1. Build the board first, module last. Strap the unused buffers, fit the 682 jumpers, make the A0 cut, and add the pull-ups before the module is plugged in.
  2. Bench power. Use an S-100 extender and feed +8 V and ground only. Check +5 V at the VCC rail and 3.3 V at P7 O10 before fitting the module. The module's USB is the easy way to check that it boots.
  3. Static bus test. With a logic analyzer or a slow Z80, read 0x61 after reset: RDY (0x04) should be set once the firmware has joined, and TXRDY (0x02) always. Check SEL* pulses only at 0x60 and 0x61, and not at 0x62.
  4. Keep VI5 low at first. The current guest driver (sys/net.c) polls RXRDY and does not claim the VI5 line. Leave GPIO12 low so the 7406 holds VI5 deasserted; bring the card up on the polled driver. Enable the interrupt only after the guest driver attaches VI5 (sys/INTR.md); an unclaimed level-asserted line is not something to discover on a live bus.
  5. Byte loop. Write a byte to 0x60 and read it back through the firmware's loopback, then exercise the FIFO status in 0x61. Verify U23: LE pulses only when SEL* is low, the D inputs are pulled down when U109 is disabled, and Q0–Q7 hold the byte after sOUT falls. Check the DI setup before sINP.
  6. Interrupt. Once the driver claims the line, confirm VI5 follows the RX FIFO: low while a frame is waiting, high after the last byte is read.
  7. Network. Join, open a UDP socket, and echo against a host. Then run rcp -r against the tree; that is the same guest binary the simulator runs, so a failure is in the bus interface, not the framing.

Timing

The Z80 gives a peripheral roughly 750 ns at 2 MHz, 375 ns at 4 MHz, and about 250 ns at 6 MHz. The read path is pre-driven: the ESP32 keeps DI0b–DI7b set to A0b ? status : rx_peek(), and U107's /OE = SEL* puts that byte on the S-100 DI lines as soon as the port is addressed. The LS245 enable is about 15 ns and the 682 plus the LVC245 add about 30 ns, well inside the 6 MHz window.

The write path no longer races the CPU. U23 latches DO0b–DO7b on sOUTb, gated by SEL*, and holds the byte after sOUT falls; the ESP32 reads it through U20 at its own speed. The only thing the firmware must catch is the sOUT edge: at 6 MHz it is about 250 ns wide, 60 cycles at 240 MHz, so core 0 sees it. The 74LS373's setup time is about 20 ns and the U24 gate adds about 10 ns, both comfortable inside the strobe.

The ESP32 must consume one latched byte before the next write to our port. The guest's own flow control and the 240 MHz loop make that easy (one byte per 667 ns at 6 MHz); if a future bus master writes the port back to back faster than that, the latch is overwritten and the byte is lost.

If the CPU needs more time, the S-100 RDY line (pin 72, on the proto board at P26.3) can be pulled low through a second 7406 until the byte is ready; that is the clean way to add wait states, but it is a second handshake and should be added only if the latch is not enough.

Firmware map

The pin map below is the logical assignment the board wiring implements. Core 0 owns the bus and never calls into lwIP; core 1 owns the stack. The two exchange bytes through single-producer/single-consumer ring buffers.

/* bus side, core 0 */
#define SEL   34   /* active low, from the 682 */
#define A0    39
#define SOUT  35
#define SINP  36
#define INT   12   /* drives the 7406; low = VI5 released */
#define DO(n) do_pin[n]   /* 32 33 25 26 27 14 13 4  */
#define DI(n) di_pin[n]   /* 16 17 5 18 19 21 22 23  */

static int prev_sout;

for (;;) {
    int sel = !gpio_get(SEL);
    int a0  =  gpio_get(A0);
    int sout = gpio_get(SOUT);

    /* read side: pre-drive the byte the CPU will take */
    if (sel)
        gpio_put8(DI, a0 ? netstat() : rx_peek());

    /* write side: U23 has latched DO; take it when sOUT falls */
    if (sel && !sout && prev_sout)
        tx_put(gpio_get8(DO));   /* U20 B -> U23 Q */
    prev_sout = sout;

    /* a NETDATA read pops the FIFO */
    if (sel && !a0 && gpio_get(SINP))
        rx_pop();

    /* level-asserted interrupt, only if the guest has claimed it */
    gpio_put(INT, rx_count() != 0 && irq_enabled);
}

The loop is illustrative: read the GPIO registers in one batch where the part allows it, and keep the core free of delays. The simulator's nic.c is the golden reference for the frame codec and socket states; only the byte channel is replaced by this loop.
